The first-timer’s guide

New to West African food? Start here.

A friendly, plain-English guide to the dishes you will see on Lizzy’s menu: what they are, how to say them and how to eat them.

How a plate works

Starch + soup or stew + protein

Most West African meals follow a simple pattern. Pick a base, pick what goes with it, pick your meat or fish. Rice dishes like jollof and waakye come as complete plates.

  1. 1The baseA swallow (fufu, banku, kenkey, eba, pounded yam) or rice, yam, plantain or attiéké.
  2. 2The soup or stewLight soup, okro soup, egusi, cassava leaf stew or pepper stew.
  3. 3The proteinGoat, chicken, beef, fish, turkey or egg.

Swallow

Fufu

Say it: FOO-foo

What it is

A soft, stretchy dough made from boiled starchy staples, most often cassava, plantain or yam, pounded or stirred until smooth.

How to eat it

Pinch off a small piece with your right hand, press a dent in it with your thumb, scoop up some soup and swallow it without much chewing.

Order it at Lizzy’s: Fufu With Goat Egusi Stew, Fufu With Chicken Stew

Swallow

Banku

Say it: BAHN-koo

What it is

Fermented corn dough and cassava dough cooked together and stirred into a smooth, slightly sour ball. A Ghanaian everyday staple.

How to eat it

Eat it like fufu, by hand with okro soup or a stew, or pair it with grilled or fried fish and hot pepper sauce.

Order it at Lizzy’s: Banku With Okro Soup Goat Meat

Swallow

Kenkey

Say it: KEN-kay

What it is

Fermented corn dough wrapped in corn husks or plantain leaves and steamed. Firmer and more sour than banku.

How to eat it

Unwrap it, break off pieces and eat it with fried fish, raw onions and pepper sauce.

Rice & beans

Waakye

Say it: WAH-chay

What it is

Rice and beans cooked together with dried sorghum leaves, which give it its deep reddish-brown color. A beloved Ghanaian plate.

How to eat it

Use a fork or spoon. It comes with a spread of sides, often egg, plantain, meat or fish and a pepper stew.

Order it at Lizzy’s: Waakye W/ Fish, Chicken, Egg, Plantains

Rice

Jollof

Say it: JAH-lof

What it is

Rice cooked in a tomato, onion and pepper base with spices until every grain turns red-orange. Loved across West Africa.

How to eat it

Fork and plate. The easiest first order if you are nervous about new textures.

Order it at Lizzy’s: Jollof Rice w/ Chicken, Fish, Egg, Plantain

Stew

Egusi

Say it: eh-GOO-see

What it is

A rich stew thickened with ground melon seeds, usually cooked with leafy greens and meat or fish.

How to eat it

Pair it with a swallow like fufu or pounded yam and eat by hand, or spoon it over rice.

Order it at Lizzy’s: Fufu With Goat Egusi Stew

Soup

Light soup

Say it: as it sounds

What it is

A thin, tomato-based Ghanaian soup, peppery and aromatic, usually made with goat, chicken or fish. Light in body, not in flavor.

How to eat it

Served in a bowl with fufu or another swallow. Pinch, dip, repeat, then finish the broth.

Cassava

Attiéké

Say it: ah-chee-EH-keh

What it is

Grated, fermented cassava, steamed into fluffy grains that look like couscous. Originally from Côte d’Ivoire.

How to eat it

Eat it with a fork, piled beside fried or grilled fish with fresh onions, tomatoes and a little chili.

Order it at Lizzy’s: Atieke (as posted: “Atieke is Ready”)

Hands on

How to eat a swallow with your hands

Eating fufu, banku or kenkey by hand is traditional and part of the pleasure. It is never required. A spoon or fork is perfectly fine.

  1. 1

    Wash up

    Start with clean hands. Traditionally you eat with your right hand.

  2. 2

    Pinch

    Pull off a bite-size piece of the dough and roll it into a small ball.

  3. 3

    Dent & dip

    Press a little dent in it with your thumb, then scoop up soup or stew.

  4. 4

    Swallow

    Swallows are not chewed much, which is how they got the name. Finish the soup with a spoon.

Heat levels

How spicy is it?

West African kitchens love fresh hot peppers, including scotch bonnet types. Heat varies by dish and by day, so use this as a general guide and ask when you order.

  • Usually gentle

    Jollof, waakye (without the pepper sauce), plain rice, boiled yam, plantain, fufu and banku on their own.

  • Warming

    Egusi, cassava leaf stew, okro soup and most stews.

  • Can be hot

    Light soup, spicy khebab and anything with pepper sauce or shito on the side.

FAQ

First-timer questions

What is a “swallow” in West African food?

A swallow is a soft dough such as fufu, banku, kenkey, eba or pounded yam. You pinch off a piece, dip it in soup or stew and swallow it with little chewing.

Do I have to eat with my hands?

No. Eating swallows by hand, with your right hand, is traditional, but a spoon or fork is always fine. Rice dishes like jollof and waakye are usually eaten with a fork.

What is the difference between fufu and banku?

Fufu is usually made from cassava, plantain or yam and is soft and mild. Banku is made from fermented corn and cassava dough, so it is firmer with a gentle sour tang.

What should a first-timer order at Lizzy's?

Start with a jollof or waakye plate if you want something familiar. For the classic experience, order fufu with goat egusi stew or banku with okro soup.

Is West African food very spicy?

It can be. Many soups and pepper sauces use hot chilies, while rice plates are often milder. Ask about heat when you order.
